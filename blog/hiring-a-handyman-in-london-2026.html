Hiring a handyman in London: the complete 2026 guide.

Published 26 July 2026 · 5 min read · By Serhii Perepechaiev, Hausio

Hiring a handyman in London should be the easiest home job you ever book: one visit, a list of small fixes, a fair hourly rate. In practice it is where people get caught out most — the shelf that never sits level, the TV mount that pulls a chunk out of a plasterboard wall, the "quick job" that quietly becomes a lost afternoon. This is a practical guide to what a good London handyman actually does in 2026, what the work really costs, and how to tell a dependable tradesperson from the noise when you search "handyman near me".

The jobs a London handyman actually gets booked for

Most call-outs are not one big task — they are a punch list of small ones. The regulars we see across the city:

  • Shelves and picture hanging — the most common request, and the one most often botched. In a Victorian conversion the walls are rarely true, so anything mounted needs proper packing and the right fixings.
  • Flat-pack and furniture assembly — IKEA wardrobes, beds and Kallax units, from £45/hour with flat rates on common models.
  • TV wall mounting — where the wall itself decides the job (more below).
  • Sealing and silicone — baths, sinks and shower trays. London's hard water chews through cheap sealant, so this comes round more often than people expect.
  • Skirting, door easing and adjustments — timber doors swell in older flats; a few millimetres planed off stops the sticking.
  • Minor plumbing and electrics — tap washers, aerators, waste traps, light fittings, a new socket faceplate. A good handyman also knows the line: anything notifiable under Part P, or any gas work, goes to a registered electrician or a Gas Safe engineer.

TV mounting is the job where London's building stock changes everything. Drill into solid Victorian brick and you want masonry plugs and a decent bracket; drill into a plasterboard stud partition — standard in newer conversions and loft rooms — and you must hit the timber studs or use proper cavity fixings, or the screen ends up on the floor. A handyman who reaches for a stud detector before the drill is telling you something.

Handyman rates in London in 2026

Honest handyman rates in London are hourly for general work and fixed for defined tasks. Ours:

  • Half-day (4 hours) £215. Full day (8 hours) £395.
  • Furniture assembly is billed at the same handyman rates.
  • TV wall mounting is fixed by screen size: up to 43" £55, 44–55" £75, 56–65" £95, 66–85" £150.

Materials — screws, fixings, sealant, a bracket if you need one — are charged at cost, never marked up. Jobs inside the Central London Congestion Zone (EC, WC, W1, SW1, SE1) carry a flat £18 access charge that covers the daily Congestion Charge, and a same-day booking adds £15. Everything is quoted before we start, so there are no surprises on the invoice.

Day rate versus hourly: which to book

The rule of thumb: book by the hour for one or two defined jobs, and by the day once the list gets long. Two or three fixes — a shelf, a blind, a dripping tap — sit comfortably inside the two-hour minimum. A flat's worth of snags, say eight to twelve small items, is a half-day at £215. A full move-in checklist, or a room being brought back to order, is where the £395 full day earns its keep, working out cheaper per hour than booking piecemeal. Send a short list at booking with a photo per job and the visit can be planned properly rather than discovered on the doorstep.

What a good handyman should have

Three things separate a professional from a bloke with a drill. First, they are vetted: every Hausio handyman is DBS-checked and interviewed in person before they set foot in a home. Second, they are insured — £2M public liability and £25k goods-in-transit — so if something goes wrong, it is covered. Third, they carry the right tools and know their limits: a good handyman turns down the jobs that legally belong to a certified electrician or a Gas Safe engineer rather than bodging them.

Red flags worth avoiding

  • Cash only, no invoice. No paper trail means no recourse.
  • A firm price for a vague list, sight unseen. Either it is padded, or corners get cut when the job runs long.
  • No insurance they can name. Ask for the cover; a real firm answers in seconds.
  • Full payment demanded upfront. You should never pay before the work is done — with Hausio it is £0 at booking, and you pay by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay only once the job is finished.
  • Drilling without checking for cables or pipes. A cheap detector prevents an expensive flood or a dead ring main.
